# Targeted prior-work review and contribution map

Search date: 9 November 2026. This is a targeted primary-source review, extending
the repository's earlier 31-source review. It is not a systematic review with
exhaustive database coverage. We opened the primary sources below, searched
specific mechanism combinations, and retained direct antecedents as well as
negative evidence about priority. All scientific priority claims remain subject
to external expert review.

## Names, tokenization and hiring

Wolfe and Caliskan (2021) examine name frequency, tokenization and contextual
representations. An and Rudinger (2023) control tokenization length and demographic
attributes in first-name substitution studies. Nayeem and Rafiei (2026) introduce
NameTrace and examine unequal name-surface support. Together these works make
clear that replacing a name is not necessarily a matched lexical intervention
[1–3]. This motivates separating native token compatibility, retained evidence
identity, admission and score outcomes. Script diversity in our panel is not a
verified racial-group annotation.

Karvonen and Marks (2025) are a direct hiring-intervention antecedent: they study
realistic contextual settings and affine concept editing [4]. Our earlier
subtractive pilots are therefore empirical baselines, not inventions of internal
fairness editing. The new compiler fits token contracts without model scores or
race directions and rejects variants with no approved witness. It addresses a
different feasibility question from learned demographic-direction editing.

## Exact isolation and erasure

Russinovich (2026), Section III-A, implements marked-span vetoes and an exact
outside-activation guarantee under declared substitution and execution
conditions [5]. This directly precedes internal marked-span noninterference.
Sabelfeld and Myers (2003) provide the classical information-flow foundations
[6]. Our earlier gather/scatter recurrence is reused, not presented as a new
unlearning algorithm.

Belrose et al. (2023) establish LEACE's specified linear concept-erasure criterion
[7]. That criterion must not be restated as erasure against every nonlinear
observer or universal name invariance. QLoRA supplies efficient frozen-base
adaptation [8], without itself supplying a fairness theorem. The present
successful mechanism trains no adapter.

## Rejection and coverage

Jones et al. (2021) show that selective classification can magnify group
disparities. Lee et al. (2021) study fair selective classification through
sufficiency [9,10]. Thus rejection-aware fairness is established prior work.
Our narrower question is how native tokenizer boundary witnesses restrict
admission for an exact single-evidence contract. The finite maximum-support
compiler, theorem and disjoint-name audit connect that feasibility constraint
to score noninterference. They do not introduce a new general principle of
selective fairness or optimize demographic error rates.

## Efficient token handling and equivalent baselines

Kim et al. (2022) prune tokens to reduce transformer computation [11]. Gathering
selected tokens is therefore not original by itself. Our compiler's evidence
policy is reviewed rather than learned importance, and our study retains the
full original named embedding stage. An external retained-ID baseline uses the
same evidence and native blocks and has the same mathematical score property.
We test that equivalence instead of claiming that internal placement alone
confers superior fairness. The official ARC dataset provides a separate standard
capability subset [12]; it is unrelated to evidence of hiring suitability.

## Claim-by-claim novelty disposition

| Candidate claim | Disposition |
|---|---|
| First activation-based hiring mitigation | Reject; [4] is direct prior work. |
| First exact marked-span isolation | Reject; [5,6] directly precede it. |
| New general concept-erasure theorem | Reject; this project neither improves nor reproduces LEACE's criterion. |
| New gather-based transformer acceleration | Reject; compact evidence execution existed in earlier project code, and token pruning has prior work. |
| Name-independent rejection guarantees racial fairness | Reject; group outcomes and role semantics are outside that implication. |
| Exact finite-panel coverage certificate for reviewed native-BPE evidence contracts | A precise contribution of this implementation; maximum-support enumeration is elementary, and field-wide priority is unproved. |
| Disjoint-name tests joining admission, aligned state equality, a strong external baseline and public reproduction | Supported description of the new empirical artifact; significance requires reviewer assessment. |
| Better hiring usefulness or universal name admission | Not established by these constructed tasks. |

## Search record

Primary queries included:

- transformer span veto noninterference variable length activation routing fairness;
- canonicalization noninterference tokenization name identity fairness;
- tokenization hiring bias names and native tokenizer boundaries;
- fairness selective classification rejection disparities;
- transformer token pruning gather scatter exact computation equivalence;
- LEACE original paper and internal hiring affine concept editing.

We did not find an exact duplicate of the implemented contract compiler in this
targeted search. That observation is weaker than establishing novelty. The
compiler uses a standard finite maximum-support calculation; the proposed
scientific value is the explicit feasibility certificate, failure analysis and
reproducible comparison in this particular setting.
